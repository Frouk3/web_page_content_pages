## What's going on?

If you aren't familiar with it, Nexus Mods is massive—it's the go-to online hub for computer game modifications and user-created content. It actually started way back in 2001 as *Morrowind Chronicles*, a fan site for *The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind*, before evolving through a few rebrands (*Morrowind Source*, *TESSource*, *TESNexus*, and *the Nexus*) into the modding giant we know today, complete with its own forums, wikis, and regular shoutouts from gaming news outlets.

Back in March 2023, Nexus Mods made its stance official: AI-generated content is allowed on the platform. Fast forward to the last few months, and AI companies have rolled out heavy-hitting models like GPT-6-Astra and Claude Sonnet 5.5. Almost immediately, dozens of repositories popped up giving these models specialized skills to reverse-engineer binary applications. That opened the floodgates for AI-generated mods. Most of them inject custom code straight into game binaries—like DLLs, ASIs, or standalone executables—while others try to reverse-engineer entire games and rewrite them in Rust, which is honestly a big reason why Rust's popularity has suddenly skyrocketed.

Modding used to be driven by genuine passion and grit. Now, practically any mod can be churned out entirely by an AI. Needless to say, this has sparked serious backlash among traditional modders who firmly believe generative AI never should have been allowed near the craft in the first place.

The first real wave of this AI slop hit *Minecraft*, with creators trying to force-port it into another game using a wildly unstable bridge that stayed broken no matter how many "fixes" they threw at it. More recently, I've noticed *GTA* mods getting uploaded at a suspiciously rapid pace. Even when these files are clearly tagged as AI-generated, it doesn't change the reality that AI is now leaning on specialized SDKs to pump out mods on autopilot. Just the other day, I saw someone trying to throw together a "quick fire rate" mod by tweaking some files, and it was painfully obvious AI was doing the heavy lifting. When they asked me for help, I politely turned them down. Why? Because it genuinely hurts to watch a game you love get buried under AI-generated garbage just because someone decided actually learning how to code is optional. At this point, all we can really do is gatekeep the craft from people who treat AI as a crutch.

## The Illness

Look, AI can be a useful tool, no doubt—but people are completely ignoring the fact that leaning on it too heavily leads to cognitive atrophy. It's a very real risk we all need to take seriously, yet we keep brushing it aside.

### What additional risks does this AI pose?
The fallout isn't just technical; it's neurological, psychological, and philosophical. From a neurological standpoint, heavy reliance on generative AI threatens to dull our minds and strip away brain plasticity. Take a four-month study from MIT, where researchers tracked 54 participants writing essays either completely unassisted, with a search engine, or with ChatGPT, all while monitoring their brain activity via EEG. While the study is still in preprint, the findings are sobering: using search engines—and especially ChatGPT—tanked cognitive engagement and "relevant cognitive load," which is the actual mental heavy lifting required to turn raw information into real knowledge. Specifically, the ChatGPT group wrote 60% faster, sure, but their cognitive load dropped by 32%. Their brain connectivity in alpha and theta waves was nearly cut in half, and a staggering 83% of the AI users couldn't even remember a passage they had just written minutes earlier.

Other research backs this up, too. A collaborative study by Qatari, Tunisian, and Italian researchers points out that heavy LLM usage puts us on a path toward cognitive decline. The neural pathways we use to structure thoughts, write, translate, and create are deep and complex. When we outsource that mental effort to an AI, we rack up a kind of "cognitive debt"—the more we automate our thinking, the less we engage our prefrontal cortex, leaving lasting effects well beyond whatever task is sitting in front of us.

### What are the psychological risks?
Generative AI is practically engineered to make us dependent on it. It talks like a human, mirrors our behavior, always seems to have the right answer, feels fun to chat with, keeps the conversation flowing effortlessly, and bends over backward to accommodate us. That kind of reliance isn't just a gateway to other issues—it's damaging on its own. It breeds social isolation, triggers a reflexive disengagement where people start asking, *"If AI can answer everything, why bother learning or thinking for myself?"* and can even leave us feeling deeply inadequate when faced with a machine's effortless efficiency. None of that paints a healthy picture for our collective mental state.

### And from a philosophical point of view?
Widespread cognitive atrophy is a massive philosophical problem on its own, yet the rabbit hole goes deeper. As this tech becomes the default—especially among younger generations—we're staring down the barrel of a total standardization of thought. Research out of the UK showed that while individual writers got a nice boost when asking ChatGPT to polish their work, the overall creativity of the group actually shrank. Then there's what it's doing to our critical thinking. A Microsoft study looking at 319 knowledge workers found a clear negative correlation ($r = -0.49$) between how often people used AI tools and their critical thinking scores on Bloom's taxonomy. Essentially, the more people trust the machine over their own brains, the quicker they offload their mental effort. Holding onto a critical mindset is crucial here, because AI doesn't just hallucinate or reinforce biases—it can actively obscure information and fake compliance ([source](https://www.polytechnique-insights.com/en/columns/neuroscience/generative-ai-the-risk-of-cognitive-atrophy/)).

## Conclusion

If we let people caught up in this "illness" keep leaning on AI to crank out mods—or anything else, for that matter—we're eventually going to hit a wall where nobody knows how to solve a complex problem on their own. Even worse, if we hand over the wheel to AI for everything, life itself risks losing its purpose, especially if it ushers in some frictionless utopia where struggle is obsolete. Is that really a future anyone wants? Struggle has always been woven into the fabric of everyday life; strip that away, and what's left? Just a hollow existence.

People still haven't grasped that they've blown the lid right off Pandora's box. Curiosity is baked into human nature, sure—but without a shred of foresight to keep us grounded, that very curiosity might end up being what ruins us.
